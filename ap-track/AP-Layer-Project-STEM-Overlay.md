# AP Layer: Project STEM Overlay

How to run the course with Project STEM's AP CSP curriculum adopted underneath it, so an audited "AP Computer Science Principles" transcript is available to any student who wants it, without changing the course for everyone else or adding much to your workload.

Two things are kept deliberately separate in this document:

1. The **AP layer**: Project STEM's syllabus, adopted as-is for the audit. This is the clearly marked, standalone piece.
2. Your **custom course**: the systems narrative, hardware labs, unplugged activities, and Python depth you already built, which sits on top and drives day-to-day teaching.

Verify current audit steps, provider endorsement, and Project STEM enrollment details before the year starts; these change.

## The model in one paragraph

Your course is the course. Project STEM is an adopted resource inside it and the document that satisfies the AP Course Audit. Every student does the same base course. The AP track is opt-in: students who want the credential work through the Project STEM units and sit the exam; younger students and anyone not testing simply do the course and earn a strong "Computer Science I" credit. No exams are required of anyone for the course grade, so there is no exam pressure on the class as a whole.

For a topic-by-topic view of how the base course already covers the AP CSP framework (all 35 topics, with a Covered, Strengthened, or Added status for each), see the AP CSP Topic Coverage Map at `ap-track/AP-CSP-Topic-Coverage.md`.

## Why this is low-effort to manage

- College Board lets AP CSP teachers **adopt an endorsed provider's pre-approved syllabus** instead of writing one. You adopt Project STEM's; authorization is typically confirmed within about two weeks.
- College Board does not mandate a curriculum for an AP course. Your own course can be the real curriculum; the adopted syllabus demonstrates scope awareness.
- Project STEM is **free** (Amazon Future Engineer sponsored), auto-grades its own coding activities and quizzes, and is self-paced. Because each student has their own MacBook, AP-track students can do the Project STEM units on their own time, which keeps the graded AP work off your plate and out of class time.

### The audit steps (once, before the year)

Good news for a solo homeschool teacher: you do not need a separate administrator to approve your form. College Board's AP Course Audit User Guide states that homeschool teachers do not need an administrator to approve their course audit form. You act as your own provider.

1. Create or sign in to a College Board professional (EPL) account and add AP Course Audit.
2. Enroll as a homeschool provider: upload documentation verifying your homeschool provider status on file with your state education agency. Your Option 3 accountability association membership serves this purpose; a signed letter of intent is also acceptable.
3. Add a new course: AP Computer Science Principles.
4. Complete the subject-specific audit form (read and initial the attestations).
5. Under course documents, choose "Adopt an Endorsed Provider Syllabus" and select Project STEM.
6. Submit. Because you adopted a pre-approved syllabus, authorization is confirmed within about two weeks, with no external faculty review and no eight-week wait. This also unlocks teacher-only AP practice exams.

Deadlines and renewal:
- The submission deadline to label a course "AP" for a given year is January 31, so set this up in the fall to be comfortably clear.
- In later years you do not resubmit forms or syllabi unless you change the course or the College Board significantly revises it. You do click to renew each August.

Your accountability association's only role is providing the membership documentation that proves your provider status. They do not sign the audit form or act as your administrator.

## The two syllabi, kept separate

### Syllabus A: the adopted AP syllabus (Project STEM)

This is Project STEM's document, submitted unchanged for the audit. Do not edit it; its value is that it is pre-approved. It covers the five AP Big Ideas and the six computational thinking practices, includes the Create Performance Task scaffolding, and is what the College Board sees. Keep the PDF from your Project STEM account on file as the authoritative AP syllabus.

### Syllabus B: your custom course (the overlay)

This is your existing plan (the main curriculum document), run as-is. It drives what happens in the room each week. AP-track students map their Project STEM progress onto it as they go; the mapping below shows how the two align.

## How Project STEM units map onto your weeks

Project STEM's AP CSP is organized around the five Big Ideas. Your course already covers all five (see Section 2 of the main plan), just in a different order and with more hands-on depth. AP-track students work the Project STEM units alongside the matching part of your course, mostly on their own MacBooks.

| AP Big Idea (Project STEM units) | Where it lands in your course | AP-track homework |
|---|---|---|
| Creative Development; intro programming | Unit 1 (Weeks 1 to 5) | Project STEM's early programming and collaboration lessons |
| Data (binary, compression, extracting information) | Unit 1 W2, plus Unit 3 | Project STEM's Data unit; this is also where your Week 2 compression topic lives |
| Algorithms and Programming | Unit 3 (the programming core) | Project STEM's algorithms and programming units, in parallel with your Python depth |
| Computing Systems and Networks | Units 2 and 4 | Project STEM's internet and systems unit; your hardware and networking labs go deeper |
| Impact of Computing | Unit 6 and the recurring ethics threads | Project STEM's impact lessons |
| Create Performance Task | Your final project window (start February) | Project STEM's mock Create Task, checklist, and template; submit via the AP Digital Portfolio by the late-April deadline |

The one place to watch alignment: Project STEM teaches programming in Python, which matches your course, but its ordering is concept-first where yours is depth-first. AP-track students should treat Project STEM as their exam-prep spine and your course as where they actually learn to program well. The AP pseudocode bridge sheet covers the one notation gap.

## Keeping younger students out of exam pressure

- The AP track is opt-in per student. Nothing about it is required for the course grade.
- No student takes the AP exam as a course requirement. Younger students do the base course only.
- The base course grade uses your existing rubric (labs, checkpoints, projects, participation). Project STEM's auto-graded AP assessments count only for students on the AP track, and only for their own AP preparation, not for the shared course grade.
- Younger students still get every benefit of the material, and a legitimate "Computer Science I" credit, without ever seeing an AP exam.

## What this adds to your workload, honestly

- One-time: the audit setup above, roughly an hour or two of forms.
- Ongoing: little. AP-track students self-pace Project STEM on their MacBooks and it auto-grades itself. Your main added job is a periodic check that AP-track students are keeping pace toward the Create Task deadline, and helping them submit through the Digital Portfolio in the spring.
- Spring only: the standing AP hour. College Board requires nine hours of supervised in-class time for the Create Task, and the regular sessions supply under three, so from Week 27 through submission you run a supervised hour after class each week for Create Task students, plus office hours by arrangement, logged with dates. Only students submitting the task attend. The weekly guides and handouts from Week 27 onward carry this.
- You are not grading AP work by hand, and you are not teaching a separate class. The AP layer rides underneath the course you were already going to teach.

## Before you commit

- Enroll as a Project STEM teacher and pull their current AP CSP syllabus PDF for the audit.
- Have your Option 3 accountability association membership documentation ready to upload as proof of homeschool provider status.
- Decide the enrollment window: AP-track students should start the Project STEM units early enough in the fall to finish the Create Task by the spring deadline, and complete the audit before the January 31 deadline.
- No administrator signature is required for a homeschool provider, so there is no external co-signer to arrange.
